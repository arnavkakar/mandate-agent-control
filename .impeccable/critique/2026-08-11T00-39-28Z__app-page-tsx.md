## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Mandate's product artifacts are specific—the three-state authorization model, policy/risk separation, human review, audit chain, and decision explainer could not be dropped into an unrelated product. The visual grammar remains category-interchangeable SaaS: dark rail, cool-gray canvas, equal white cards, KPI tiles, status pills, violet accent, and generic icon grids. The authority boundary should become the dominant spatial metaphor rather than content placed inside generic cards.

**Deterministic scan:** Three advisory `design-system-color` findings appear in `app/page.tsx`: `#0B8B75`, `#D97706`, and `#3B82F6` on agent identity colors. The first two drift from documented approved/review tokens; the blue is an undocumented one-off identity color. No blocking detector findings occurred.

**Visual evidence:** The hosted site exposed only the ChatGPT sign-in gateway. The local build exposed the new API-backed authentication screen, but account creation was deliberately not performed during the read-only assessment. Mutable script injection was unavailable, so no reliable user-visible overlay exists.

## Overall Impression

The product thesis is unusually clear and the decision explainer is the strongest, most ownable surface. The interface currently looks more complete than it behaves: its demo-era instant actions, tiny type, and missing persistent-state feedback undermine the trust created by the policy model. The largest opportunity is to turn Mandate from a generic dashboard containing authorization data into an unmistakable financial authority console organized around what needs action, why, and what changes afterward.

## What's Working

1. Explainability is genuinely designed in: policy checks, risk factors, decisions, and the non-LLM authority boundary remain distinct.
2. Decision semantics are consistent and redundant across tables, queue, simulator, and drawer.
3. Simulation truthfulness is excellent: “Simulation mode,” “No money moves,” and the no-card/no-bank boundary prevent false impressions.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] High-stakes actions have no safe decision ceremony

**Why it matters:** Approve, decline, pause, approval-all, and mandate activation can change financial authority immediately without budget-after-impact, mandate version, reviewer note, confirmation, or recovery.

**Fix:** Add evidence-rich confirmation/review steps, explicit destructive separation, recoverable success feedback, and budget/mandate context.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Typography is below a production legibility floor

**Why it matters:** Pervasive 7–10px copy makes tables, audit evidence, risk factors, scopes, and controls difficult to scan and inaccessible to low-vision users.

**Fix:** Use at least 12px metadata, 14–16px body, and 13–14px controls; strengthen contrast and reserve uppercase labels for genuine system metadata.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

### [P1] API-backed reality has no complete state model

**Why it matters:** Authentication and persistent data introduce loading, stale, empty, permission, validation, session-expired, network-error, retry, and success states that the demo never represented.

**Fix:** Define the full state matrix per view, preserve layout with loading treatments, and give every failure an inline recovery path.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Card repetition flattens hierarchy and product character

**Why it matters:** Five equal KPI tiles, five equal risk cards, and repeated icon-heading-text grids force scanning and make Mandate resemble a generic admin template.

**Fix:** Make authority requiring attention the primary object, consolidate secondary metrics, turn risk into one analytical composition, and prefer dividers/structured rows to more cards.

**Suggested commands:** `/impeccable distill`, `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] Operator efficiency and implementation truth are weak

**Why it matters:** There are no stable detail routes, sorting, bulk actions, saved filters, shortcuts, or mandate history, while inert connector/actions look enabled.

**Fix:** Add efficient ledger navigation and mark planned capabilities truthfully as unavailable rather than actionable.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

**Morgan, small-business owner:** Overview does not immediately answer “Am I safe and what needs action?” Approval omits budget-after-impact and why approval is safe. Risk and integrity terminology is unexplained.

**Alex, power operator:** Cannot sort, bulk-resolve, save filters, deep-link an agent/transaction, compare mandate versions, or navigate common work by keyboard.

**Priya, security reviewer:** Audit evidence lacks full actor, mandate version, policy input/output, timezone, and reviewer note. “Architecture-ready” can be mistaken for implemented integration.

**Jordan, keyboard/low-vision user:** Clickable table rows lack keyboard semantics; the drawer lacks dialog/focus behavior; several icon buttons lack names; 7–10px text is practically unreadable.

## Cognitive Load and Emotional Journey

Four of eight cognitive-load checks fail: single focus, chunking, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure. The nav exposes eight destinations; Overview and Risk each present five equal objects; Connections exposes identity, API, outcomes, code, ownership, and adapters at once. Entry is calm and credible, and the decision drawer creates the best peak. The emotional valley is the approval moment: the most consequential action has the least ceremony and ends abruptly without reassurance or recovery.

## Minor Observations

- Overview attention rows imply clickability inconsistently.
- Mobile hides the New Mandate label; the menu needs an accessible name.
- KPI tiles remain two columns at 520px and will struggle with zoom/localization.
- Planned connectors and the Production switch must not imply live capability.
- Risk scores need thresholds or a legend.
- Create Mandate needs ambiguity, validation, diff, and activation feedback.
- Agent identity colors should use documented tokens.

4. Which health, blocking, and integrity claims can expose real provenance?
